How I work

Rigorous, direct and designed for use.

I am most comfortable with unstructured problems. I establish the decision that has to be made, identify the minimum evidence needed, and keep a strategic view while working through the detail. Clients value an honest assessment, including when the promised model or conclusion is not yet defensible.

01

Start with the decision

Define what must be decided, by whom and by when before building a model or collecting more data.

02

Follow the whole pathway

Look across organisations, incentives, patient flow, workforce, data and funding rather than optimising one isolated component.

03

Challenge constructively

Test assumptions, distinguish evidence from aspiration and provide a practical alternative rather than simply identifying a flaw.

04

Leave something usable

Build tools, decision frameworks and operating approaches that can be embedded, tested and improved — not just a presentation.

Selected work

These examples show the type of problem, the role I played and the decision or system change the work supported.

Current · complex programme evaluation

Equitable access to HIV prevention

Leading a multi-site evaluation of clinic, community, women-facing and digital PrEP pathways. The work brings together patient-level outcomes, pathway attribution, implementation evidence and proportionate value-for-money analysis.

The central challenge is not simply to count activity, but to understand whether different approaches reach underserved populations and move people credibly from access into uptake and continued use.

Decision Which approaches are sufficiently effective, equitable and workable to continue, adapt or scale.
Funding & payment design

Integrated Sexual Health Tariff

Led the development of a pathway-based funding model for integrated sexual health services. The model combined time-driven costing of more than 140 clinical pathways with top-down provider cost analysis and existing national activity data.

I then led road-testing and implementation, including a web-based solution that enabled rapid onboarding without major capital expenditure. The programme continued through the transfer of sexual-health commissioning from the NHS to local authorities.

Outcome More transparent and equitable payment, stronger incentives for integrated and digital delivery, and reported system savings exceeding £50 million.
HIV policy & system review

UN AIDS Fast-Track Cities — London

Led an assets, gaps and opportunities review of London’s route towards international HIV goals. The work combined more than 120 interviews, service-user storytelling, quantitative analysis and health-economic assessment.

It brought together perspectives from people living with HIV, clinicians, commissioners, local authorities, community organisations and national bodies.

Outcome A system-wide evidence base and recommendations for the Mayor of London, NHS England, Public Health England and London Councils.
Cross-government system reform

National Sexual Assault Services Review

Co-led national work across NHS England, the Ministry of Justice and the Home Office. We mapped pathways through 43 Sexual Assault Referral Centres and more than 100 voluntary-sector organisations, examining cost drivers, workforce, hand-offs and failure points from forensic response through to the wider justice pathway.

The work translated detailed frontline evidence into a structure senior decision-makers could use to negotiate responsibilities and funding.

Outcome Evidence supporting a clearer cross-government responsibility matrix, new funding arrangements and a more coherent operating model.
National analytical leadership

Joint Biosecurity Centre / UKHSA

Following nearly 1,000 hours as a frontline clinical contact tracer, I moved into national analytical leadership during the COVID-19 response. I led a multidisciplinary team of epidemiologists, data scientists, product designers and engineers developing cluster-detection and transmission-analysis products.

The role required constant negotiation between scientific rigour, operational usefulness and the speed of ministerial decision-making.

Outcome Analytical products and evidence designed for both local public-health action and rapid national policy decisions.
Frontline evidence into policy

NHS Test and Trace issue analysis

Used direct tracing experience to compile a detailed issue log covering practical failures in the national pathway, data quality, household linkage, timeliness and user experience. A rapid analysis identified more than 13 million excess calls associated with unlinked household records.

The work was escalated to senior programme leadership and became part of the evidence base I later brought into government analytical roles.

Value Converted frontline experience into a structured diagnosis of system and data problems requiring national attention.
Pathway economics

Diabetes care, King’s Health Partners

Led a health-economics and strategy review of hospital and community diabetes pathways. The work combined pathway mapping, activity analysis and stakeholder workshops to identify costs hidden by coding problems, fragmented care and poor coordination between providers.

Outcome A clearer view of the true pathway cost and practical options for shifting appropriate care into community settings.
Value for money

National Chlamydia Screening Programme

Developed a practical cost and cost-effectiveness tool covering screening and partner notification, alongside value-for-money evidence for national decision-makers and commissioners.

The analysis shifted attention away from the simple volume of tests towards the cost of identifying and managing positive cases.

Outcome Contributed to the case for programme continuation and gave commissioners a more meaningful basis for local decision-making.
About Bella

Analysis with a view of the whole system.

Bella Grant is the founder and director of Pathway Analytics. Her career spans investment banking, equity research, strategy consulting, health economics, national public-health analysis and political campaigning.

She is particularly interested in work where finance, service delivery and equity intersect: how resources flow, who is reached, where people are lost from a pathway, and whether a programme is producing enough value to justify continuation or scale.

In 2024 she stood as the Liberal Democrat parliamentary candidate for Fareham and Waterlooville, building a campaign from scratch and receiving 9,533 votes. She also serves in local civic and voluntary roles.
